# Debate Preparation Portfolio

## Basic Information
- **Topic (Motion)**: AI use should be allowed in all KIT classes.
- **Side**: Opposition (反対派)
- **Format**: Oxford-style debate

---

## 1. Core Arguments (Opposing "All Classes" Allowance)

### Argument 1: Risk to Fundamental Skill Acquisition
- **Claim**: Allowing AI in ALL classes, especially fundamental courses (e.g., introductory programming, mathematics, basic CAD), prevents students from developing essential problem-solving and logical thinking skills.
- **Evidence/Reasoning**: Learning requires friction and trial-and-error. Relying on AI outputs removes the critical cognitive process needed to build foundational engineering expertise.

### Argument 2: Breakdown of Academic Integrity and Fair Assessment
- **Claim**: It becomes impossible for professors to fairly evaluate individual student abilities when AI usage is permitted without restriction across every course.
- **Evidence/Reasoning**: AI-generated code, essays, and reports blur the boundary between a student's actual competence and AI assistance, leading to unfair grading for students who put in independent effort.

### Argument 3: The Need for Subject-Specific Flexibility Rather Than a Blankets Rule
- **Claim**: A blanket policy ("all classes") ignores the diverse pedagogical goals of different subjects.
- **Evidence/Reasoning**: Decisions should be made per class by the course instructors. Advanced research projects benefit from AI, but foundational lab work and introductory exams require manual mastery.

---

## 2. Anticipated Rebuttals (賛成派への反論準備)

- **Pros' Claim**: "AI tools are ubiquitous in industry; restricting them disadvantages students."
  - **Counter-Rebuttal**: Industry engineers need deep subject-matter knowledge to evaluate, correct, and audit AI outputs. Without unassisted foundational training, students cannot verify if AI outputs are safe or correct.
